# I Tested 6 Free AI Voice Generators — Only 1 Is Free to Publish (October 2026)

For a faceless channel, the voice *is* the channel. A robotic narrator kills retention in the first three seconds, and the wrong free voice can do worse than that: it can get your video demonetized, or leave you with audio you are not allowed to download at all.

So I tested six free AI voice generators — not on how nice they sound in a demo, but on the three questions that actually matter to a creator: how much do you get for free, can you download what you make, and are you legally allowed to publish it in a video that earns money?

Here is the short version before the detail: the best-sounding free voice in this test bans commercial use. The biggest free allowance hides behind a developer setup wall. And two of the six tools will not even let you take your audio home.

## The three traps

Every "free" AI voice tool has at least one catch. In this test, the catches fell into three groups.

**Trap 1: the best voice bans monetized videos.** The most natural-sounding voice in the test comes with a free tier that is explicitly non-commercial. You can play with it. You cannot earn with it.

**Trap 2: the biggest allowance hides behind a setup wall.** One tool gives away far more free characters than any other — but only after you set up a cloud billing account. If you cannot get through the setup, the allowance might as well not exist.

**Trap 3: you cannot download what you make.** Two of the six tools will happily read text aloud to you and then refuse to give you the audio file. For a YouTuber, that is not a free tier. It is a demo.

Keep those three traps in mind. They decide the ranking more than voice quality does.

## 1. ElevenLabs — the best voice, and the worst free deal for a creator who earns

ElevenLabs sounds the best of the six. If this were a listening contest, it would win. It is not a listening contest.

The free tier gives you 10,000 credits a month. That sounds generous until you read the licence: the free tier is **non-commercial**. You are not allowed to use free-tier voices in a monetized video. The moment your video earns money — ads, sponsorships, any of it — you are breaking the terms.

That makes ElevenLabs the most dangerous tool in this test, precisely because it is the most tempting. A beginner hears the best voice, builds a channel on it, and only discovers the licence problem when the channel starts earning.

**Verdict:** Best voice here. Unusable for a monetized channel on the free tier. If you ever pay, it becomes a different conversation. Free, it is a no.

## 2. Google Cloud Text-to-Speech — the biggest free allowance, behind a setup wall

Google gives away 4 million free characters a month. That is far more than any other tool in this test. On paper, this is the winner by a mile.

In practice, there is a wall: billing must be enabled on your Google Cloud account before you can use it, even though the first 4 million characters are free. That means creating a cloud project, adding a payment method, and navigating a developer console. For a non-technical creator, that setup is the whole test — and many will give up before hearing a single word.

If you can get through the setup, this is the most powerful free voice stack available. If you cannot, it does not matter how generous the allowance is.

**Verdict:** Biggest free allowance in the test. Worth it if you can clear the setup wall; a non-starter if you cannot.

## 3. Microsoft Edge Read Aloud / Azure F0 — unlimited reading, zero voiceover workflow

Edge's Read Aloud will read anything to you, for free, with no limit that matters. Azure's F0 tier is the same family of voices, offered as a developer service.

The problem is workflow. Read Aloud is a reading tool, not a voiceover tool. There is no export button, no download, no way to take the audio into an editor. Azure F0 can produce audio, but it is a developer service — API keys, code, setup — not a creator's tool.

For listening to articles, this is genuinely good. For making a video, it is a dead end on the free tier.

**Verdict:** Great for listening. Not a voiceover workflow. Cross it off for YouTube.

## 4. TTSMaker — the one you can actually publish for free

TTSMaker is the only tool in this test that passes all three checks. It gives you 20,000 characters a week, free. The allowance renews every week, so it is not a one-time trial. You can download your audio as an MP3. And the free tier is legal to use in monetized videos.

The voice is not the most natural of the six. ElevenLabs sounds better. But TTSMaker is the only free tier that is downloadable, renewable, and commercially usable all at once — and for a creator, that combination matters more than a slightly smoother voice.

20,000 characters a week covers a weekly video for most faceless channels. If you publish more often, you will need to plan around the weekly cap, or pair it with Google's free tier.

**Verdict:** The winner. Not the prettiest voice — the only free one you can actually publish.

## 5. NaturalReader — generous listening, nothing you can upload

NaturalReader is generous in exactly the way a creator does not need. It will read long documents aloud, for free, with decent voices. But on the free tier, there is no MP3 export. You can listen. You cannot download.

That is the same trap as Edge Read Aloud, in a nicer package. A listening tool is not a production tool, no matter how good the voices sound in your headphones.

**Verdict:** Good listener, useless for production on free. Skip it for YouTube.

## 6. Murf — a demo, not a tier

Murf gives you 10 minutes on the free tier. Ten minutes, no downloads, no commercial rights. That is not a free tier for a creator — it is a trial designed to show you the product and then ask for your card.

The voices are good, and the editor is polished. None of that matters if you cannot take the audio home or earn from it.

**Verdict:** A demo, not a tier. Test the voices if you like, then move on.

## The ranking

1. **TTSMaker** — the only free tier that is downloadable, renewable weekly, and legal to monetize. The practical winner.
2. **Google Cloud Text-to-Speech** — the biggest allowance by far, if you can get through the billing setup.
3. **Microsoft Edge Read Aloud / Azure F0** — unlimited reading, but no voiceover workflow.
4. **ElevenLabs** — the best voice in the test, disqualified for monetized videos on the free tier.
5. **NaturalReader** — good listening, no export.
6. **Murf** — 10 minutes you cannot take home.

And the free stack for October 2026: TTSMaker for your weekly videos, Google Cloud as the backup once you clear the setup. That combination covers most faceless channels without spending anything.

## FAQ

**Can I use ElevenLabs free voices on a monetized YouTube channel?**

No. The ElevenLabs free tier is non-commercial. Using it in a video that earns money breaks the licence. If your channel is monetized — or you plan for it to be — do not build on the free tier.

**What is the best free AI voice for YouTube?**

The best free voice you can legally publish is TTSMaker: 20,000 characters a week, downloadable, renewable, and commercially usable. ElevenLabs sounds better, but its free tier bans commercial use. Google Cloud gives you far more characters, but only after you set up a billing account.

**Do free tiers change?**

Yes, constantly. Every figure in this post was checked against the tools' official pricing pages on 24 October 2026. Credit amounts, download rules, and licences change without warning — check the pricing page before you rely on any of them.
