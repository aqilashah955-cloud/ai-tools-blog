# A Simple Content-Writing Workflow with AI

AI tools can write a full article in seconds. That speed is tempting — and it is also the trap. An article written entirely by AI is usually generic, sometimes wrong, and never in your voice. The workflow below keeps the speed and fixes the problems: the AI drafts, you decide.

## Step 1: Plan before you open the tool

Write three lines on paper before you type anything:

- **Who is this for?** One reader, described plainly. Example: a beginner who has never used an AI tool.
- **What should they be able to do after reading?** One outcome. Example: write a better prompt.
- **One point you actually know.** Something from your own experience, not something the AI will invent.

If you cannot write these three lines, you are not ready to draft. The AI cannot plan for you — it will guess, and its guess will be generic.

## Step 2: Outline, don't draft

Ask the AI for an outline, not an article. Or write the outline yourself in five minutes — headings only, no sentences.

A good outline for a tutorial has four to six sections, each answering one question the reader has. Check the outline against your plan: does every section serve the reader's outcome? Cut any section that does not.

## Step 3: Draft one section at a time

Feed the AI one section at a time, with the context from your plan. A good prompt for a section sounds like this:

"Write the section 'Give context' for a beginner's guide to prompts. Audience: complete beginners. About 150 words, simple English, one example."

One section at a time keeps the quality up and makes editing easy. A full article in one prompt is hard to fix; a single section is easy to rewrite.

## Step 4: Edit in your own voice

This is the step most people skip, and it is the step that matters most. Read the draft aloud and fix:

- **Sentences that sound like nobody talks.** Rewrite them the way you would say them.
- **Examples that are not real.** Replace them with examples from your own experience.
- **Filler.** Cut any sentence that does not help the reader do the thing.
- **Your point.** Add the one thing you know that the AI could not have known. That is what makes the piece yours.

A good rule: if you removed your name from the article, would a reader still know it was yours? If not, it needs more of you in it.

## Step 5: Fact-check before publishing

AI tools can state wrong things confidently — names, dates, prices, free-tier limits. Before you publish:

- Check every number, name, and date against the original source.
- Open every link and confirm it goes where it says it goes.
- If a claim matters and you cannot verify it, cut it. A shorter honest article beats a longer wrong one.

## A checklist for every post

- [ ] Reader and outcome written down before drafting
- [ ] Outline checked against the plan
- [ ] Drafted one section at a time
- [ ] Read aloud and edited in my voice
- [ ] At least one real example from my experience
- [ ] Every fact and link checked
- [ ] Nothing published that I have not read fully

## How long does this take?

The first time, about an hour for a short tutorial: 10 minutes planning, 10 minutes outlining, 20 minutes drafting in sections, 20 minutes editing and checking. It gets faster with practice — but the editing and checking steps never go away. They are the work. The AI just makes the first draft faster.
