# A Productivity Setup for Beginners Using Free AI Tools

Most productivity advice fails for the same reason: it asks you to install ten apps and follow a complicated system. A beginner does not need a system. A beginner needs three things that work together, and a small routine for using them.

## The three pieces

### 1. One place for notes

Pick one notes app and put everything in it: ideas, meeting notes, things to read later, drafts. One place, always searchable. It does not matter which app — the free notes app already on your phone works fine. What matters is that there is only one place, so you always know where something is.

Keep notes short. A note is a sentence or a list, not an essay. If a note grows long, it is really a draft — move it to your writing workflow.

### 2. One list for tasks

One list, reviewed once a day. Each task is a verb with a clear finish: "Email the homework list to Class 5", not "homework". Three tasks at the top are today's work; everything else waits below. When a task is done, mark it done and move on — do not rewrite the list.

### 3. One AI helper

One AI chat tool, used for three jobs only:

- **Drafting:** first versions of emails, messages, and short texts, which you then edit.
- **Summarising:** turning a long article or long notes into five bullet points.
- **Explaining:** "Explain this in simple words" for anything you do not understand yet.

Three jobs. That is the whole AI part of the setup. If you catch yourself asking it to do a fourth, fifth, and sixth job, you are adding complexity, not productivity.

## The daily routine (10 minutes)

**Morning (5 minutes):**

- Open your task list. Pick the three tasks for today.
- Open your notes. Check anything you captured yesterday that needs an action.

**Evening (5 minutes):**

- Mark today's tasks done or move them to tomorrow — no guilt, just a decision.
- Capture anything unfinished into your notes so your head is empty.
- Write tomorrow's top three tasks if you already know them.

Ten minutes a day keeps the system alive. Without the routine, the apps are just installed software.

## The weekly review (20 minutes, once a week)

Pick one quiet time each week — Friday afternoon, Sunday evening, whatever fits — and do three things:

1. Delete or file notes you no longer need. A notes app full of old clutter stops being searchable.
2. Look at the tasks you keep moving forward. Either do them, break them into a smaller first step, or delete them. A task that moves forward for three weeks is not a task; it is a wish.
3. Ask your AI helper to summarise your week's notes into five lines. Read the summary. It shows you what you actually spent your time on.

## Rules that keep it simple

- **One of each.** One notes app, one task list, one AI helper. Adding a second of anything is the first step toward a system you will abandon.
- **Free tiers first.** Free versions of these tools cover a beginner completely. Pay only when you hit a limit you can name.
- **Paper is allowed.** If a paper list works better for you than an app, use paper. The routine matters, not the tool.
- **Never let the tool decide.** The AI drafts; you decide. The list reminds; you choose. The moment a tool starts deciding for you, it has stopped helping.

## Start today

Set up the three pieces now: open your notes app and write one note, open your task list and add three tasks, open your AI helper and ask it to summarise something you read today. Then do the five-minute evening routine tonight. That is the whole setup — the rest is just doing it again tomorrow.
