# I Tested Pixverse + 5 'Free' AI Video Generators — Only 1 Is Actually Free (November 2026)

Welcome back to the monthly re-test — the series where I re-check what free AI video tools actually give you, so you never waste time on a dead free tier. Last month I re-tested five "free" AI video generators, and most of you asked for the same thing in the comments: add Pixverse. So this month the lineup is six. I re-checked every single one of them again, because free tiers move without warning.

Here is the short version before the detail: one tool launched a brand-new flagship model. One tool's free tier has effectively vanished from its own pricing page. And the buzziest new name in AI video? Its free deal is thinner than it looks. Every figure below was re-verified in October 2026 against the tools' own pricing pages, help pages and terms.

## The ranking

1. **Google Flow (Veo 3.1)** — the only one actually free to publish
2. **Kling 4.0** — a real daily allowance, but watermarked and non-commercial
3. **Dreamina × Seedance 2.5** — the biggest daily budget, with the murkiest terms
4. **Pixverse (V6)** — fun, fast and famous, but 30 credits a day rarely buys a clip
5. **Pika** — no watermark, but zero free credits
6. **Luma** — the free tier may not exist anymore

Four out of six watermark free output. But the only number that matters: of these six tools, only one — Flow — gives you free clips you can legally publish on a monetized channel. Same verdict as October. Different lineup underneath it.

## 1. Google Flow (Veo 3.1) — still #1

I re-checked Flow against Google's own help page, and nothing moved: 50 free credits a day, refreshed when you make your first generation of the day. Flow runs Veo 3.1, with native sound and dialogue. Lite clips cost 10 credits, Fast clips cost 20, and the top Quality mode costs 100 — out of reach of any single free day. So one free day buys you five Lite clips, or two Fast ones.

Two catches from October still apply: unused credits vanish (no rollover), and at peak hours free accounts can be paused behind paying subscribers. But here is the headline, and it is the whole video: no visible watermark — just Google's invisible SynthID tag — and Google's terms don't ban commercial use of Flow output. It is the only tool in this test whose free clips you can legally publish on a monetized channel.

**Verdict:** In November 2026, still the best free deal in AI video — and it is not close.

## 2. Kling 4.0 — new flagship, same handcuffs

Kling 4.0 launched on October 15 — 10-bit HDR, up to 30 seconds in a single pass, lip-sync, native audio, available to everyone. The free tier is still 66 credits a day, expiring in 24 hours. What changed is the billing: Kling now charges by the second, roughly 6 to 12 credits per second — so on the newest model, those same 66 credits evaporate faster than ever.

Free exports are still watermarked, still capped around 540p, and still personal use only — commercial rights start at $10 a month. One open question: whether free accounts actually get the 4.0 flagship or are defaulted to an older model — check that in the app before you plan around it.

**Verdict:** A stronger model, wearing the same handcuffs.

## 3. Dreamina × Seedance 2.5 — biggest budget, murkiest terms

Dreamina runs Seedance 2.5 now — up to 30 seconds per pass, native audio, near the top of the independent leaderboards. Dreamina's own guide says a free account gets about 120 credits a day — the biggest daily number in this test, enough for one to three short clips.

But the fine print is a mess. Dreamina's own blog says two different things about the watermark in two different places, and independent reviewers consistently report that free downloads are watermarked. The commercial-use position is just as unclear — paid plans clearly grant it; free is not clearly safe. And no official page states the free resolution cap, so I won't quote one.

**Verdict:** The best free engine got even better. The free terms are still a maze. If you use it, plan on a watermark and keep it personal.

## 4. Pixverse (V6) — the one you asked for

Pixverse is everywhere right now: 79 million installs, 2 million in the last 30 days, and its PixLight film contest — offering $300,000 — closes on October 31. The app runs PixVerse V6, its flagship model, with native audio and camera control. So what does free actually get you?

Sixty credits when you sign up, then 30 free credits a day — and those daily credits expire at midnight, no rollover. Here is the catch nobody mentions: a five-second clip costs roughly 25 to 40 credits. So your daily 30 often does not buy a single finished clip. Free output is capped at 540p, it carries a visible watermark, and it is non-commercial — you cannot legally use it in a video that earns money. And read the terms: Pixverse takes a perpetual, sublicensable licence to use your content for training, unless you opt out.

**Verdict:** Fun, fast, and famous — but 30 credits a day is a demo, not a free tier.

## 5. Pika — still zero

I checked Pika's official pricing again. The free plan still includes zero monthly credits — you can only buy credit packs. The one nice line in the table is unchanged: no watermark, on any tier. A clean, watermark-free tier… that generates nothing.

Two things did change around it. First, Pika is now a multi-model hub — inside Pika you can now buy generations on other people's models, like Seedance 2.5 and Wan 3.0. Second, the paid Starter plan — $10 a month — now lists a commercial licence on the official page, which older guides said it didn't have.

**Verdict:** Literally nothing changed on free. Still zero.

## 6. Luma — the free tier that isn't there

The Dream Machine name is retired — it is just Luma now, running models called Ray 3.2 and Ray 3.14. Open Luma's pricing page today and you will find no free plan at all. The cheapest way in is Plus, at $30 a month. Older guides describe a legacy free plan — eight draft-mode videos a month, watermarked, non-commercial, five seconds max — but whether that still works in the app is unverified, and Luma's own current page doesn't offer it.

And the training catch is the worst in this test: free generations grant Luma rights to train on your content, and the no-training guarantee is an Enterprise-only feature.

**Verdict:** The weakest free in this lineup. The free tier that isn't there.

## The free stack for November 2026

So — did free AI video change in November? Yes. Just not where the headlines said. The models leapt forward again — Kling 4.0, Seedance 2.5, Pixverse V6. But the free tiers barely moved: same credits, same watermarks, same expiry clocks.

Here is the stack: Flow for your hero clips — the ones that need sound and a clean export. Dreamina for volume, when you need drafts. Kling when you want its particular look. And expect a watermark on everything except Flow.
